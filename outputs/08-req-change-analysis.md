# 08-Requirement Change Analysis

## 1. Affected Entities and Attributes

Based on the transition from the Phase 1 schema to the Phase 2 operating conditions, the following entities and attributes have been modified or introduced to support the new business logic:

- **ROLE (New Entity):** Extracted from the `USERS` table into a dedicated reference table containing `role_id` (PK) and `role_name`.

- **SPACE_TYPE (New Entity):** Introduced to classify spaces into predefined categories. Each `SPACE` now references a `space_type_id` instead of storing the space type directly.

- **AUTO_USAGE_POLICY (New Entity):** Created to define which combinations of `space_type_id` and `role_id` are eligible for automatic booking processing.

- **USERS (Updated):** The `role` string attribute was replaced with a `role_id` Foreign Key linking to the new `ROLE` table.

- **SPACE (Updated):** The `space_type` attribute was replaced with the `space_type_id` Foreign Key referencing `SPACE_TYPE`. The `usage_policy` attribute was removed because automatic booking eligibility is now defined by `AUTO_USAGE_POLICY`.

- **BOOKING_DECISION (Replaces BOOKING_APPROVAL):** Renamed to support both manual and automatic booking decisions. The entity adds `is_automatic` to indicate whether the decision was generated by the system, retains `is_approved` as the decision outcome, replaces `approved_by` with `decided_by_staff` (FK) for manually reviewed bookings, and merges approval and rejection notes into a unified `decision_reason` attribute.

- **MAINTENANCE_RECORD (Updated):** Added an `impact_level` attribute to distinguish between `out_of_service` and `advisory` maintenance.

- **BOOKING_NOTIFICATION (New Entity):** Introduced to record maintenance-related notifications associated with bookings. Each notification links a `booking_id` (FK) with a `maintenance_id` (FK), records a `notification_type`, and stores the `notification_time`. Multiple notifications for the same booking and maintenance record are supported provided they represent different notification types.

- **USAGE_SESSION (Updated):** Now references `decision_id` (FK) rather than `booking_id` directly, ensuring a session can only stem from a formalized booking decision.

---

## 2. Affected Relationships

The structural changes introduce the following new relationships to the database:

- **ROLE and USERS:** A 1:N relationship (`assigned_to`) linking roles to users.

- **SPACE_TYPE and SPACE:** A 1:N relationship (`categorizes`) linking each space to one predefined space type.

- **SPACE_TYPE and AUTO_USAGE_POLICY:** A 1:N relationship (`auto_policy`) defining which space types support automatic booking for eligible roles.

- **ROLE and AUTO_USAGE_POLICY:** A 1:N relationship (`eligible_role`) identifying which user roles qualify for automatic booking of a particular space type.

- **BOOKING_REQUEST and BOOKING_DECISION:** A 1:0..1 relationship (`results_in`) where a booking request produces at most one booking decision.

- **USERS and BOOKING_DECISION:** A 1:N relationship (`decides`) recording the staff member responsible for manual booking decisions.

- **BOOKING_REQUEST and BOOKING_NOTIFICATION:** A 1:N relationship (`receives`) where a booking may receive multiple maintenance-related notifications.

- **MAINTENANCE_RECORD and BOOKING_NOTIFICATION:** A 1:N relationship (`generates`) where a maintenance record may generate notifications for multiple affected bookings.

## 3. Updated Business Rules

- **Maintenance Impact Rule:** A space under maintenance with an `out_of_service` impact level cannot be booked for any overlapping time period.

- **Maintenance Advisory Rule:** A space with an `advisory` impact level can still be booked, but the system must notify the requester of all active advisories. Each generated notification is recorded in the `BOOKING_NOTIFICATION` relation.

- **Maintenance Escalation:** The impact level of a maintenance record can be escalated (`advisory` → `out_of_service`) or downgraded while open. If escalated to `out_of_service`, the system must identify already-approved bookings that overlap the maintenance period and generate the appropriate notification records for affected bookings.

- **Automatic Booking Policy:** A user's eligibility for automatic booking is determined by matching the user's `role_id` and the requested space's `space_type_id` against the `AUTO_USAGE_POLICY` relation.

- **Instant Booking & Unified Decision Tracking:** For selected space types, booking requests that satisfy the applicable automatic usage policy are approved automatically at submission time. Every booking that reaches a final decision, whether through automatic approval or manual review, generates a `BOOKING_DECISION` record to ensure a consistent audit trail.

- **Strict Non-Overlap Rule:** The system must ensure that two approved bookings cannot use the same space during overlapping time periods, regardless of whether the bookings are created through instant booking or staff approval.

## 4. Concurrency Conflicts Caused by Booking and Approval Operations

The introduction of instant booking, automatic approval, and concurrent staff operations increases the likelihood of transaction conflicts. Multiple users, facility staff, and backend processes may simultaneously access or modify the same booking or space. Without proper concurrency control, these concurrent operations may violate business rules or produce inconsistent system states.

### 4.1 Concurrent Maintenance Updates

Booking operations may execute concurrently with maintenance updates on the same space. While one transaction is validating availability, another may create, modify, complete, or change the impact level of a maintenance record.

Without proper synchronization, a booking may be approved even though the space has just become unavailable due to maintenance, or a maintenance operation may incorrectly affect bookings that are being processed concurrently.

---

### 4.2 Concurrent Approval Workflows

Booking decisions may be processed simultaneously by different approval workflows.

These conflicts include:

- **Human ↔ Human:** multiple staff members process the same booking or competing bookings concurrently.
- **Human ↔ Machine:** a staff member manually processes a booking while the automatic approval procedure is evaluating the same request.
- **Machine ↔ Machine:** multiple backend processes execute automatic booking operations for the same space type at the same time.

Without concurrency control, bookings may receive conflicting decisions, inconsistent approval metadata, or violate the automatic booking policies and booking conflict rules defined by the system.
